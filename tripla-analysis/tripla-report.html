Critical analysis

Strengths

  • Three-pillar, full-stack integrator model — one relationship spans infra, cyber and GRC instead of a point solution.
  • Proprietary IP in all three pillars (CyberWatch, Trisky, Autoritas) — better margin and stickiness than a pure reseller.
  • Deep manufacturer bench: Cloudflare, Okta, Qualys, BeyondTrust, Trend Micro, Huawei, Check Point, Illumio, TXOne, Cohesity, Netwrix.
  • Unusually broad certification spread (ISO 27001/22301/20000-1, IAPP, EC-Council, HCIE) — a real RFP scoring advantage.
  • Brazil-native 24x7 NOC/SOC — timezone, language and LGPD data-residency edge over offshore MSSPs.
  • Diversified blue-chip proof points: iFood, Volkswagen, SIMPAR, multiple Unimed federations, Hermes Pardini/Fleury, State Grid.
  • Active thought leadership (press citing Roberto Toscani on AI governance) feeds top-of-funnel credibility.

Weaknesses / risks

  • Structural dependence on third-party licensing — a manufacturer going direct, or losing a program to a rival integrator, is a real renewal risk.
  • Visible partner churn: the "Varonis → Netwrix migration" page signals a manufacturer switch worth having a ready answer for.
  • Heavy use of anonymized case studies weakens otherwise strong industrial DR/DDoS proof points.
  • 25+ solution lines spanning cyber, GRC, VDI, video surveillance and even battery storage risk a "jack of all trades" read — BESS and CCTV sit oddly next to a "cybersecurity enterprise" headline.
  • No visible pricing/packaging tier — every deal is a bespoke cycle, no anchor for smaller or faster-moving prospects.
  • Domestic-only footprint, with no visible LatAm/global-account story for multinational HQ buyers.

Market positioning

Tripla reads as a Brazilian "high level" systems integrator / MSSP / GRC-as-a-service player for enterprise and upper-mid-market accounts in regulated, operationally-critical verticals — healthcare, financial services, energy, industry, logistics, retail, call center/BPO. The pitch is consolidation: one vendor across availability, protection and compliance, anchored by three owned products and a large certification bench. Its natural competitive set is Brazilian integrators/MSSPs, boutique LGPD/GRC consultancies, and global MSSPs expanding into Brazil — and owning CyberWatch, Trisky and Autoritas is the piece a pure reseller can't easily copy.

Overall impression

A credibly-scaled (130+ clients, 10+ years, 70% YoY growth), certification-heavy integrator with a coherent three-pillar narrative and real proprietary IP — stronger on breadth and delivery credibility than on crisp positioning. The clearest near-term opportunity is tightening the story (the energy-storage and video lines dilute an otherwise sharp "cybersecurity + compliance" narrative) and converting more anonymized cases into named references as NDAs allow.
